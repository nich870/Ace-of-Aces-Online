What each knob controls
Knob		     Effect						                                Raise it to...				   	    Lower it to...
dealt	      Value of landing a hit	    		    	| make the AI hunt shots more aggressively	     make it less bloodthirsty
taken	       Cost of getting hit	         	    	|	make it more risk-averse	                  make it more reckless
tailBonus   Value of being on your tail (before any shot)   	|	make it chase position harder		make it care less about setup, more about the actual moment of firing
tailedPenalty	Cost of being tailed (before any shot)	    	| make it flee sooner when threatened	       make it linger in bad spots longer
shortRangeBonus	Preference for closing to short range (1–36)	|	make it a close-in brawler	           make it comfortable sniping at long range
noise	     Random jitter on every move evaluation	    	| make it sloppier/more human, more beatable	      make it play closer to its theoretical best every turn
worstFrac	How much of the outcome distribution it judges itself by 
		(1.0 = full average, smaller = judges by its worst-case slice only)  make it more paranoid/cautious	     make it more of a gambler, judging moves by their average outcome instead of worst case
Some alternative presets to try

Trigger-happy Ace — instead of cautious-but-lethal, an Ace that presses the attack recklessly:

javascript
ace: { dealt: 22, taken: 6, tailBonus: 4, tailedPenalty: 1.5, shortRangeBonus: 3, noise: 2, worstFrac: 1.0 }

Turtle Veteran — if you flip the complaint and want a version that plays it safe rather than aggressive (good "easy defensive" mode):

javascript
veteran: { dealt: 9, taken: 14, tailBonus: 1.5, tailedPenalty: 4, shortRangeBonus: -0.5, noise: 3, worstFrac: 1.0 }

(Note the negative shortRangeBonus — it actively prefers staying at range.)

Sniper Ace — hangs back, only closes in when it has an edge, picks at you from distance rather than brawling:

javascript
ace: { dealt: 18, taken: 9, tailBonus: 2, tailedPenalty: 3, shortRangeBonus: -1, noise: 1, worstFrac: 0.5 }

Chaotic Rookie — if even the current Rookie feels too competent in its "smart" 30% of moves:

(You could also just raise the 0.7 random-move chance in aiChooseFullMove/aiChooseFullMoveKnowingPlayerCategory — e.g., to 0.85 — for an even easier Rookie without touching weights at all.)

A tip for dialing it in

The fastest way to feel the effect of a change without replaying a bunch of games yourself: bump noise way up temporarily (like 15–20) for whichever difficulty you're testing — it'll make several games' worth of variety show up quickly, and you can watch whether the AI still "feels" aggressive/passive even with that randomness layered on top, which tells you whether the underlying weights are doing what you want before you dial the noise back down.
